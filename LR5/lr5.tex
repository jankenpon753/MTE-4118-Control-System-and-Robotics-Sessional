\subsection*{Overview}
\addcontentsline{toc}{subsection}{Overview}
This experiment examines root-locus and time-domain behavior of an armature-controlled DC motor regulated by P, PI, PD, and PID controllers. Analysis uses the motor's electromechanical model, classical controller forms, and closed-loop pole placement via root-locus \cite{Ogata,Franklin}.

\subsection*{DC motor model}
\addcontentsline{toc}{subsection}{DC motor model}
The armature-controlled motor couples electrical and mechanical dynamics:
\[
  L\frac{d i_a(t)}{dt} + R i_a(t) + K_e \omega(t) = v_a(t),
  \qquad
  J\frac{d\omega(t)}{dt} + B\omega(t) = K_t i_a(t),
\]
where $v_a$ is armature voltage, $i_a$ armature current, $\omega$ angular speed, and $R, L, J, B, K_e, K_t$ are motor constants. Taking Laplace transforms and eliminating $I_a(s)$ yields the transfer function from armature voltage to speed:
\[
  G(s)\;\equiv\;\frac{\Omega(s)}{V_a(s)}
  =\frac{K_t}{(J s + B)(L s + R) + K_e K_t},
\]
which follows standard electromechanical modelling procedures \cite{Nise,Ogata}.

\subsection*{Controller structures}
\addcontentsline{toc}{subsection}{Controller structures}
Standard linear controllers used are:
\[
  C_{PI}(s)=K_p+\frac{K_i}{s}=\frac{K_p\big(s+\frac{K_i}{K_p}\big)}{s},
\]
\[
  C_{PD}(s)=K_p+K_d s = K_d\left(s+\frac{K_p}{K_d}\right).
\]
\[
  C_{PID}(s)=K_p+\frac{K_i}{s}+K_d s =\frac{K_d\left(s^2 + s\left(\frac{K_p}{K_d}\right) + \frac{K_i}{K_d}\right)}{s}.
\]
These controller forms and their tuning implications are discussed in classical texts and PID literature \cite{Franklin,astrom}.

\subsection*{Qualitative effects of actions}
\addcontentsline{toc}{subsection}{Qualitative effects}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Proportional (P): raises gain — faster response, reduces but doesn't eliminate steady‑state error for type‑0 systems.
  \item Integral (I): adds a pole at 0 — eliminates step steady‑state error, at expense of phase margin.
  \item Derivative (D): adds phase lead — improves damping and lowers overshoot, but amplifies high‑frequency noise.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Closed-loop form and design approach}
\addcontentsline{toc}{subsection}{Closed-loop form}
For unity feedback the closed-loop transfer from reference $R(s)$ to output $\Omega(s)$ is
\[
  T(s)=\frac{C(s)G(s)}{1+C(s)G(s)},
\]
with characteristic equation 1+C(s)G(s)=0. Design proceeds by inspecting the root locus of G(s) (and the effect of controller zeros/poles) and selecting controller gains so closed‑loop poles meet desired transient specifications \cite{Ogata,Franklin}.

\subsection*{Design criteria and motor data}
\addcontentsline{toc}{subsection}{Design criteria and motor data}
Motor parameters used in this lab:
\[
  J=0.01,\quad B=0.1,\quad K_e=K_t=0.01,\quad R=1,\quad L=0.5.
\]
Performance targets:
settling time $T_s \approx 1$ s (2\% criterion), and maximum overshoot $M_p < 5\%$ \cite{MATLAB}.

\subsection*{Time-response metrics}
\addcontentsline{toc}{subsection}{Time-response metrics}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Rise time $t_r$: time to go from 10\% to 90\% of final value.
  \item Peak time $t_p$: time to the first maximum peak.
  \item Settling time $T_s$: time to remain within a 2\% (or 5\%) band.
  \item Percent overshoot $M_p$: $\dfrac{\text{peak}-\text{steady}}{\text{steady}}\times100\%$.
  \item Steady‑state error $e_{ss}$: final deviation from reference (reduced by integral action).
\end{itemize}

\section*{Discussion}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Discussion}
Root‑locus guided controller tuning produced the expected trade‑offs between speed and accuracy. Key observations:
\begin{itemize}
  \item PI: removed steady‑state error; $Ts \approx 0.50 \mathrm{s}$, $M_p \approx 1.66\%$.
  \item PD: fastest transients but residual offset; $Ts \approx 0.03 \mathrm{s}$, $final \approx 0.976$.
  \item PID: balanced performance—fast settling, low overshoot, near‑zero steady‑state error; $Ts \approx 0.04 \mathrm{s}$, $M_p \approx 1.34\%$.
\end{itemize}
Recommendation: add a low‑pass filter on the derivative path and evaluate robustness to parameter variations.

\section*{Conclusion}
\addcontentsline{toc}{subsection}{Conclusion}
Design objectives (overshoot < 5\% and acceptable settling) were met by the tuned controllers: the PI controller effectively eliminated steady‑state error while maintaining low overshoot and a short settling time; the PD controller accelerated the transient response and reduced overshoot but exhibited a residual steady‑state error due to lack of integral action; and the PID controller provided a balanced compromise with fast settling, low overshoot, and minimal steady‑state error. Recommendations: implement derivative action with a practical low‑pass filter to limit noise amplification and evaluate robustness to plant uncertainty and parameter variation in future work.
